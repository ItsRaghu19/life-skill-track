# Listening and Active Communication

### Question 1: What are the steps/strategies to do Active Listening?

1. **Pay full attention:** Focus on the speaker and avoid distractions or thinking about something else.
2. **Avoid interrupting:** Allow the speaker to finish before responding.
3. **Show interest:** Use phrases like "I see", "That's interesting" and "I understand" to encourage the speaker.
4. **Use positive body language:** Maintain eye contact, nod your head and show that you are interested.
5. **Take notes:** Write down important points during meetings or discussions so you don't miss anything.
6. **Paraphrase:** Repeat the speaker's message in your own words to confirm that you understood it correctly.
7. **Ask questions:** Ask relevant questions whenever something is unclear.
8. **Avoid judging:** Listen with an open mind instead of making assumptions or preparing your response while the other person is speaking.


### Question 2: According to Fisher's model, what are the key points of Reflective Listening?

According to Fisher's model, the important points of reflective listening are:

1. **Listen without interrupting:** Allow the speaker to express their complete thoughts and opinions.
2. **Remember important information:** Pay attention to the key points and details shared by the speaker.
3. **Take notes:** Write down important information to avoid forgetting anything.
4. **Summarize the message:** Repeat the main points in your own words to show what you understood.
5. **Keep an open mind:** Be willing to understand different opinions, even when you disagree.
6. **Show genuine interest:** Give the speaker your attention and make them feel comfortable sharing their thoughts.
7. **Confirm your understanding:** Ask the speaker whether your interpretation or summary is correct.


### Question 3: What are the obstacles in your listening process?

1. Sometimes I get distracted by my own thoughts instead of focusing on the speaker.
2. I sometimes start thinking about my response before the other person finishes speaking.
3. I find it difficult to concentrate when the conversation is too long.
4. Sometimes I make assumptions without listening to the complete information.
5. I may lose track of small but important details during technical discussions.
6. Sometimes I get impatient when someone takes too long to explain something.

### Question 4: What can you do to improve your listening?

1. Give my complete attention to the speaker and avoid distractions.
2. Stop interrupting and allow others to finish their sentences.
3. Take notes during important meetings and technical discussions.
4. Ask questions whenever I don't understand something.
5. Summarize the important points to confirm my understanding.
6. Avoid making assumptions and listen with an open mind.
7. Pay attention to the speaker's body language and emotions.

## Types of Communication

There are four main communication styles:

- **Passive:** Not expressing our opinions, feelings or needs because we don't want to upset others.
- **Aggressive:** Expressing our opinions without considering other people's feelings.
- **Passive-aggressive:** Hiding our frustration and expressing it indirectly through sarcasm, taunts or silence.
- **Assertive:** Expressing our thoughts and needs clearly while respecting other people's opinions and feelings.

### Aggressive Communication

1. When someone repeatedly makes the same mistake, I sometimes become impatient and raise my voice.
2. When things don't go as planned, I sometimes get frustrated and respond without thinking.
3. When someone doesn't understand my point even after multiple explanations, I sometimes become irritated.
4. When I am under pressure or stressed, I sometimes focus more on proving my point than understanding the other person.
5. Sometimes I interrupt others because I feel that my point needs to be understood immediately.

### Passive-Aggressive Communication

### Question 7: When do you switch into Passive-Aggressive communication styles in your day-to-day life?

1. When someone disappoints me, I sometimes avoid talking to them instead of explaining what bothered me.
2. When I feel ignored, I sometimes use sarcasm to express my frustration.
3. When someone asks me for help but I don't want to do it, I sometimes agree but show my frustration through my actions.
4. Sometimes I make indirect comments instead of directly addressing the issue.
5. When I am upset with someone, I sometimes expect them to understand my feelings without explaining them.

### Assertive Communication

### Question 8: How can you make your communication assertive? What are a few steps you can apply in your own life?

After watching the videos, I understand that assertive communication means expressing my thoughts, feelings and needs confidently without disrespecting others.

I can improve my communication by following these steps:

1. **Understand my feelings:** Identify what I am actually feeling before responding to someone.
2. **Understand my needs:** Think about what I need from a situation instead of expecting others to understand it automatically.
3. **Use "I" statements:** Express my feelings without blaming others. For example, instead of saying "You never listen to me", I can say "I feel that my opinions are not being heard."
4. **Start with small situations:** Practise speaking up in simple situations before handling difficult conversations.
5. **Improve my body language:** Maintain eye contact, use a confident tone and keep a comfortable posture while speaking.
6. **Speak up early:** Address problems when they happen instead of keeping my frustration inside until it becomes an argument.
7. **Respect others' opinions:** Listen to different perspectives and understand that disagreeing doesn't mean someone is wrong.
8. **Learn to say no:** Politely refuse requests when I cannot take additional responsibilities instead of agreeing to everything.
