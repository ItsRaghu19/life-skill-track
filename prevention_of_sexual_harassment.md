# Prevention of Sexual Harassment (POSH)

## 1. What kinds of behaviour cause sexual harassment?

Sexual harassment is **unwelcome behaviour of a sexual nature**. It can be verbal, non-verbal, physical or online. Examples include:

- Unwanted touching, hugging, physical advances or repeated requests for dates.
- Sexual jokes, comments, gestures, staring or remarks about someone's body.
- Asking for sexual favours or offering work benefits in return for them.
- Showing or sharing sexually explicit pictures, videos or messages.
- Sending unwanted sexual messages or making inappropriate comments online.
- Spreading sexual rumours or asking intrusive questions about someone's private life.

A workplace should also take bullying and harassment seriously. Repeated insults, humiliation, intimidation, threats, exclusion or misuse of authority can create a hostile and unsafe work environment, even when the behaviour is not sexual.

## 2. What would you do if you faced or witnessed such behaviour?

1. **Make safety the priority:** Move away and seek help if the situation feels unsafe.
2. **Make it clear, if safe:** Tell the person that the behaviour is unwelcome and ask them to stop. Do not put yourself at risk to do this.
3. **Record what happened:** Note the date, time, place, people involved and any witnesses.
4. **Keep evidence:** Save relevant emails, messages or screenshots. Avoid deleting or altering them.
5. **Report it:** Raise the concern with the company's POSH Internal Committee (IC/ICC), HR or the designated contact, following the company's reporting process.
6. **If I witness it:** I would not ignore it. I would check on the affected person, listen without blaming them, offer support and report the incident through the proper channel.
7. **Respect confidentiality:** Do not gossip or share details unnecessarily, and do not tolerate retaliation against anyone who raises a concern.

I would take both single serious incidents and repeated behaviour seriously. I would also treat colleagues respectfully, maintain professional boundaries and avoid behaviour that could make someone uncomfortable.
